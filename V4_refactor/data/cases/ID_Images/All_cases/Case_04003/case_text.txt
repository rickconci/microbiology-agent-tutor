History of Present Illness
Medical Student Curriculum Case Summary: A woman with weight loss and cough.
Four months before this presentation, a woman in her thirties was seen at an outside medical facility with a one-week history of nonproductive cough and night sweats . Chest radiography and computed tomography (CT) showed left upper lobe consolidation and scattered bilateral pulmonary nodules. The patient was treated with levofloxacin and the symptoms abated.
In the following two months, she noted a poor appetite and a 20-pound weight loss . Three weeks before this presentation, left pleuritic chest pain and cough productive of yellow sputum occurred. There was no recurrence of fever, chills or night sweats.
Past Medical History
She had a history of anemia.
Medications
Medications included iron sulfate.
Epidemiological History
The patient was and had last visited more than five years earlier. There was no known exposure to tuberculosis and no animal exposure.
Physical Examination
The patient appeared tired, with a normal temperature and vital signs. 0xygen saturation was 100% while breathing ambient air. Examination of the lungs revealed fine crackles over the left upper lung field; no wheezes were heard. There was no hepatosplenomegaly or rash, and the remainder of the examination was normal.
Studies
The white blood cell count was 9,900 cells/mm³ (reference range 4500-11,000); with a normal differential (59% polymorphonuclear leukocytes, 2% bands, 30% lymphocytes, 6% monocytes, 3% eosinophils), hematocrit 32.3% (reference range 36.0-46.0 in women), mean corpuscular volume (MCV) 68 fL (reference range 80-100), and platelets 350,000/mm³. Results of electrolytes and liver function testing were normal. Radiography of the chest is shown in Figures 1 and 2 .

Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Chest radiography revealed a large left upper lobe mass with cavitation and CT scanning showed a left upper lobe mass with cavitation and bilateral pulmonary nodules ( Figures 1 through 4 above).
A purified protein derivative (PPD) Mantoux test was administered and a reaction of 15 centimeters induration recorded. Induced sputum revealed 4+ acid-fast bacilli by Auramine O staining. After three weeks , sputum cultures grew Mycobacterium tuberculosis ( Figure 5 ), which was sensitive to all antibiotics tested.
Figure 5. Acid-fast (Ziehl-Neelson) stain from culture.
Treatment and Followup
Isoniazid, rifampin, pyrazinamide, and ethambutol were administered initially; the ethambutol was discontinued after the sensitivity results were known. Pyrazinamide was administered for the first two months and isoniazid and rifampin for 6 months. Follow-up sputum obtained at 2 months revealed no acid-fast bacilli on staining and culture.
Discussion
The classic presentation for pulmonary tuberculosis includes upper lobe consolidation, weight loss and night sweats. Those features were present in this case, however the diagnosis may have been delayed by the empiric therapy for pneumonia with levofloxacin, which has activity against Mycobacterium tuberculosis .
In a study of patients with culture-confirmed tuberculosis, initial empiric therapy with a fluoroquinolone was associated with a delay in initiation of antituberculous therapy [Dooley et al 2002]. Thus, clinicians should consider the diagnosis of tuberculosis in patients with upper lobe cavitation or infiltrates, especially if they have epidemiologic risk factors for acquisition or reactivation of Mycobacterium tuberculosis .
Final Diagnosis
Pulmonary infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis .
