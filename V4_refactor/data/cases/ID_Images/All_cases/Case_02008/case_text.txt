Medical Student Curriculum Case Summary: A woman developed fever after visiting Haiti.
A woman in her sixties, living in the United States, returned for a visit to her family in Haiti. One week following her return to the United States, she developed fevers to 103°F (39.4°C), rigors, lethargy, and shortness of breath.

Physical Examination
Her temperature was 103°F (39.4°C), and the pulse was 100 beats per minute. She was , oriented but lethargic, and had bilateral on lung examination.
Studies
The hematocrit was 20% . The blood smear is shown in Figure 1 . Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) was 1900 U/L. Chest radiography showed bilateral diffuse air space disease.

Clinical Course Prior to Diagnosis
She rapidly developed worsening mental status, anuric renal failure, and respiratory failure with bilateral pulmonary infiltrates.
Diagnostic Procedure(s) and Result(s)
Examination of the peripheral blood smear showed numerous intra-erythrocytic ring forms including some with double rings ( Figure 1 ), gametocytes ( Figure 2 ), and schizonts ( Figure 3 ) consistent with Plasmodium falciparum . Estimated initial parasitemia was 20%.

Figure 3. Blood smear with schizont.
Treatment and Followup
Severe malaria is a common cause of fever in returning travelers. In this patient, the high parasitemia and possible cerebral, pulmonary, and renal involvement required intubation, dialysis, blood transfusions, and prolonged support in an intensive care unit. She was treated with intravenous and exchange transfusion.
Discussion
Common causes of fever in travelers returning from Haiti include malaria, dengue, and typhoid fever. This patient had severe malaria due to Plasmodium falciparum . Because P. falciparum can infect red blood cells of all ages, the level of parasitemia may be higher with P. falciparum malaria than with P. vivax or P. ovale , which infect young red blood cells. P. falciparum induces expression of knobs on the red blood cell surface which mediate cytoadherence to endothelial cells and microvascular disease. The result of this microvascular disease may be cerebral, renal, and pulmonary complications, as seen in this case.
Final Diagnosis
Severe malaria due to Plasmodium falciparum.
